Inside Product

Making Arc easier to enter, easier to build on, and easier to scale.

The Product team's current focus is not just new features. It is the connective tissue that makes Arc easier to enter, easier to build on, and easier to scale.

This issue highlights six efforts that work together: Arc Composer as the next layer of governed automation, Arc Portal as the front door, the roadmap in Jira as the visibility layer, intake as the pathway for new ideas, the design system as the shared experience layer, and the out-of-the-box modules available today.

01Arc Composer
Governed Automation

Arc Composer: the next layer of governed automation

Arc Composer takes a written plan — starting with incentive compensation — and turns it into a configured, running scenario inside Arc, without engineering rebuilding the logic for every change. It is the layer that closes the gap between what business teams write down and what Arc actually executes. The current product plan targets an internal Alpha in [Placeholder: confirm Alpha month] 2026 and a design-partner Beta in September 2026, with broader release planning still in formation. The work has moved beyond concept framing into detailed engineering enablers in Jira, including scenario workflows, a shared Composer repository, Postgres setup, Auth0 and Portal Staging integration, Databricks service-principal work, and GitHub alignment. For the broader company, the message is straightforward: position Composer as part of the Arc ecosystem, not as a standalone AI demo.

02Arc Portal
The Front Door

Arc Portal Rollout: what to expect and how to engage

Arc Portal is the central entry point for Arc experiences. Nuvalent is the first production client (now live), with Scholar Rock in active migration and StealthBio, Servier, and Celcuity queued behind them. Follow-on capability releases continue through the May–June window and into the rest of 2026. The practical ask for the broader company: use the Portal pathway as the starting point for new Arc conversations, and expect more repeatable FAQs, onboarding guidance, and enablement materials as rollout continues.

What's next on Portal
  • Calendar module — first scoped for Nuvalent go-live, then reusable across all portal clients (HIGH priority on the idea board).
  • Data management tiles — landing-page tiles showing pipeline status, last refresh, and data quality at a glance.
  • Centralized user management — provisioning across Databricks, Sigma, AQM, and Warehouse UI through one pane of glass.
  • App-bar features — global search, alerts and notifications, waffle-style app switcher.
  • UI standardisation — close remaining UX gaps against the design system.

Add your idea to the Arc Portal idea board, or comment on an existing item to push it up the queue.

05Design System
Shared Experience Layer

Design System Rollout: one shared experience layer

The design system gives Product, Studio, and prototype teams one experience layer, with consistency, speed, and clearer guardrails as the goals. Product discussions this month reinforced that the system should first support Arc Studio configuration work, internal product builds, and client-facing prototypes, with controlled adoption rather than an open-ended custom-app promise. That matters because a shared design system helps teams reduce design drift, move faster, and make digital work feel recognizably Beghou from the first click. The design system is working infrastructure for product delivery — used on the next prototype, the next Studio module, the next client demo.

Reader Action Use the design system on the next relevant product, prototype, or demo and send gaps back to Product and Design so the system keeps improving.
