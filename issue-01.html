Inside Product

Making Arc easier to enter, easier to build on, and easier to scale.

The Product team's current focus is not just new features. It is the connective tissue that makes Arc easier to enter, easier to build on, and easier to scale.

This issue highlights five efforts that work together: Arc Portal as the front door, Arc Composer as the next layer of governed automation, the roadmap in Jira as the visibility layer, intake as the pathway for new ideas, and the design system as the shared experience layer.

01Arc Portal
The Front Door

Arc Portal Rollout: what to expect and how to engage

Arc Portal is being positioned as the central entry point for Arc experiences, with the current roadmap in Jira showing an MVP in the May–June window and follow-on increments for calendar capabilities, alerts and notifications, and third-party integrations later in 2026. The practical ask for the broader company is simple: use the Portal pathway as the starting point for new Arc conversations, and expect more repeatable FAQs, onboarding guidance, and enablement materials as rollout continues. The goal is a simpler, more consistent way for internal teams to explain Arc and guide clients to the right experience.

Portal FAQ
  • Portal is becoming the front door for Arc, with additional capabilities planned in staged follow-on releases.
  • Teams should start with the shared Portal pathway rather than side-channel explanations.
  • Client teams should expect clearer FAQs, onboarding guidance, and a more repeatable entry experience.

02Arc Composer
Governed Automation

Arc Composer: the next layer of governed automation

Arc Composer is being built as a governed configuration layer for Arc, starting with incentive compensation scenarios. The current product plan targets an internal Alpha this summer and a design-partner Beta in September 2026, with broader release planning still in formation. Just as important, the work has moved beyond concept framing into detailed engineering enablers in Jira, including scenario workflows, a shared Composer repository, Postgres setup, Auth0 and Portal Staging integration, Databricks service-principal work, and GitHub alignment. For the broader company, the message is straightforward: position Composer as part of the Arc ecosystem, not as a standalone AI demo.

Composer FAQ
  • Arc Composer is a governed Arc layer for turning plan intent into configured execution workflows.
  • The current plan points to Alpha this summer and a design-partner Beta in September 2026.
  • It should be positioned as part of the Arc ecosystem, not as a general AI demo.

Action Use the Composer FAQ before discussing timelines or external positioning, and route readiness questions back to the Composer team.

03Roadmap
The Visibility Layer

Roadmap and Jira: making priorities more visible

The roadmap is becoming easier to see and easier to discuss because more of the work is now being captured in Jira instead of living only in meetings. Current priorities include Arc Portal, the Arc Composer Beta path, the Arc Studio React UI upgrade with out-of-the-box modules, Arc DE operational tooling, and Alignment migration into the web experience. That shift gives teams a clearer view of sequencing, dependencies, and what is actively moving now.

Action Use the roadmap board and Jira discussions to understand active priorities and where work is headed next.

04Intake
Pathway for New Ideas

A better intake path for ideas and change requests

The intake form is becoming the shared pathway for product ideas, module requests, and change requests. The most important change is not a headline number. It is that more requests can now enter the same review rhythm, which makes them easier to compare, prioritize, and shape consistently. Because the connected list source was not accessible from this environment, this issue avoids publishing an unverified submission count and keeps the message focused on the improved path itself.

Reader
Action Submit product ideas and change requests through the intake form so they can be reviewed in the same operating rhythm.

05Design System
Shared Experience Layer

Design System Rollout: one shared experience layer

The design system rollout is about consistency, speed, and clearer guardrails. Product discussions this month reinforced that the system should first support Arc Studio configuration work, internal product builds, and client-facing prototypes, with controlled adoption rather than an open-ended custom-app promise. That matters because a shared design system helps teams reduce design drift, move faster, and make digital work feel recognizably Beghou from the first click. The message for the broader company is that this is working infrastructure for better product delivery, not just a visual refresh.
